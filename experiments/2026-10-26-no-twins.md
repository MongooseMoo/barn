4449b485 carrying the same db/store/direct_txn_bench_test.go (six direct-transaction
benchmarks over the 6-deep, 8-verbs-per-object chain of benchChainStore). Allocation counts
do not depend on machine load. ns/op does; it is shown, both trees were run back to back on a
loaded machine (62% at the start), and it is not a gate.

Where the read allocations come from (`-memprofile`, alloc_space, FindProperty and
FindCallableVerb benchmarks): walkProperty 48%, walkVerb 51%, objIDSet.add 14% (cumulative
inside the walks). That is the private scratch the design gives the shared direct txn
(design 3.2 item 4): a fresh propScratch/verbScratch per walk whose queue, visited list and
step record grow from empty. The step record exists only for the per-transaction memo, which
is off for the direct txn.

The writes allocate the private writer (StoreTxn and its objects map), the staged write maps,
writesByObject's map and objectWrites, and a private copy of each written object at staging
plus the published clone at publication, where the old twin cloned once.

### The wide direct write (fixed by B+J+N, below)

BenchmarkDirectDefinePropertyWide (added with W-D') defines and deletes a property on an
object with 1,000 children through the direct transaction: 2,000 descendant slot writes per
op. It was measured on all three trees and at the 3c head before the cost fixes (21dcfe6f,
uncommitted bench copy in a scratch worktree):

| | pre-3b | master | 3c before fixes | final |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| allocs/op | 14,085 | 14,085 | 31,296 | 25,282 |
| B/op | 2,378,805 | 2,378,705 | 6,184,365 | 7,368,514 |

Profiles: `-memprofilerate 1`, `pprof -top`, alloc_objects, 13 ops on the branch and 24 on
master (both include setup).

- **Master:** one republish clone per child per write: cloneObjectNonVerbState 124,219,
  propTable.clone 123,148, withHistoryEntry 49,050, propTable.put 25,001.
- **Branch:** on top of the one publish clone, a staging copy of each child's property table:
  - propTable.clone 137,162: staging plus publish
  - privatizeCached 27,027
  - propTable.put 27,002
  - writesByObject 27,040: one props slice per child
  - lazySet of propertyWrites 13,286

The staging copy of the properties is the facet the write changes, so W-A2 cannot share it.
Revision 2 rejected W-B (publish the staged copy). The wide-define design (scratchpad
design-3c-wide-define.md) removed the staging instead; see the next section.

### W-D' size bound

writerKeepLimit = 256 (store_txn.go). After a write whose staged entries or cached objects
exceed it, writeNow drops the writer instead of keeping its maps (maps never shrink). Why 256:

- The benchmarked ordinary direct writes stage one to three entries.
- 256 keeps the writer for any write that touches up to 256 objects or slots.

The bytes a kept 256-entry writer retains were not measured. Before B, the wide benchmark
(2,000 staged slots per op) took the drop path on every write; after B it stages one entry
and caches one object, so the writer is kept.

## The wide define and the commit sizing (design: scratchpad design-3c-wide-define.md)

Two commits, after the history rebuild (design section 8), on base e83980da, then rebased
onto 11a17d8c (#544: engine test and docs only; db/store unchanged since c537d6c9):

- B+J+N (f1602317, then c8e6c1b5): the writeNow writer stages only the definer's define or
  definition delete; publishStagedLocked's store-side walk gives each child its slot, and
  joins it in writeSet.joined (id -> image) rather than a 344-byte entry; a clone leaves an
  empty chparentChildren nil.

  min(stagedEntries, max(24, len(tx.objects))) entries, not min(stagedEntries, 24).
